> The launch campaign for the `@machinaapp` account. Day 1 is App Store launch
> day. Six weeks, 31 posts: one launch thread, four arcs, nine singles, six
> Sundays. Brand voice, English only. Every product claim below was checked
> against the shipped code on 2026-09-21 (build 1333); the few that need a live
> screenshot or a real number are marked `[fill]` and must never be invented.

> House rules this document obeys, and every post must keep obeying: the name is
> `Machina` (never "Machina AI"); "second brain" and "AI-powered" appear nowhere;
> Machina is never called a bookmark manager (the word "bookmarks" names only

> **Decisions this document honors.** From the 2026-09-02 session: Day 1 is
> App Store launch day, not a pre-launch build-in-public run; brand voice, not

## 1. The strategy in one page

**What the campaign has to do.** Turn a launch into a habit of following. A
launch post gets one day of attention. A story gets six weeks of it. So the
posts are not 31 announcements. They are one argument, told in acts, with the
product demonstrating each step instead of describing it.

**The argument.**

1. You save things everywhere, and then you never see them again. (The problem
   is fragmentation, not clutter. This is the founder letter's story and the
   launch film's act one.)
2. Machina is one place for all of it, from every app, in one tap, and for
   everything you already saved before today. (Capture. The hero.)
3. Every save is read, filed, and connected on its own. (Understanding. The
   thing no folder app does.)
4. So later you can just ask, and the answer is yours because the sources are.
   (Recall. The payoff.)
5. And you can trust it: your data, your library, a real free plan, honest when
   it doesn't know. (Trust. What turns a download into a habit.)

**The shape: arcs, singles, Sundays.**

- An **arc** is three or four posts on consecutive weekdays, numbered 1/3 or
  1/4, each one a complete tweet on its own and together a chapter. Arcs are
  how a timeline gets a shape people come back for. Four arcs, one per week,
  weeks two to five, in the order the product performs: capture, understanding,
  Ask, trust.
- A **single** is one post, one moment, with no number. Singles carry the beats
  that do not need a chapter (the problem, the anatomy of a card, search,
  collections, Hebrew, sharing, the belief, the numbers) and they sit on the
  days between arcs so the feed never reads as a manual.
- **Sunday** is the spine. The product writes its synthesis on Sunday, so the
  account posts something synthesis-shaped on Sunday, every week, on the day it
  actually happens. By week three a follower knows Sunday means synthesis.

| Week | Arc or theme | Days | Posts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Launch**: the whole argument, one beat a day (thread + singles) | 1 to 8 | T1 to T7 |
| 2 | Arc **Save from anywhere** (article, video, screenshots, everything you already saved) | 9 to 13 | T8 to T12 |
| 3 | Arc **What a card knows** (summary, filing, connections) + search single | 15 to 20 | T13 to T17 |
| 4 | Arc **Ask** (only your library, the citation, the honest no) + collections single | 22 to 27 | T18 to T22 |
| 5 | Arc **Trust** (yours, free, everywhere) + Hebrew single | 29 to 34 | T23 to T27 |
| 6 | **The bigger idea**: sharing, the belief, the numbers, the close (singles) | 36 to 41 | T28 to T31 |

**Cadence.** Daily in launch week. Then four posts a week (three arc days plus
Sunday), five in week two because capture is the hero and its arc has four
parts. Never two arc parts on one day. Never a feature post on a Sunday.

**Voice.** Brand voice: plain, specific, confident, unhurried. Machina is the
subject of most sentences. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no hashtags, no
"we're thrilled". Short sentences. Concrete nouns (a sourdough schedule, a
40-minute talk) instead of categories ("content"). The product is never called
smart, magical, or powerful; the post shows what it did and lets the reader
draw the adjective. One `finally` per campaign surface, so the tone stays wry
and never tips into scolding (BRANDING D-6).

**Self-contained rule.** Every tweet is read cold. X shows a reply, an arc
part, or a thread tweet on its own, out of order, to people who have never
seen the account. So every tweet names Machina and says, in a clause, what it
is (one place for everything you save, from any app), and every arc part
carries the arc name and its number. Nothing may depend on the tweet before
it. The IDs below (T1 to T31, thread parts T1.1 to T1.5) are internal, for
discussing the copy; they never appear in a post.

**Honesty rule (new, 2026-09-21).** Machina has a free plan and a Pro plan
(§7.1): free is 100 saves and 20 questions a month, the Sunday synthesis as a
teaser, no YouTube watching, no curated digests; every new account gets Pro for
14 days with nothing to cancel. So no post says "free" next to a Pro feature,
no post says "unlimited", and the two Pro-only beats (the video, the full
synthesis) say so in the tweet or its first comment, in plain words, the first
time they appear. A follower who installs after reading a post must find
exactly what the post showed.

**Media rule.** Every post ships with one image or one clip. A text-only post
is allowed exactly twice (the belief post and the community ask). Clips are
under 20 seconds, vertical or square, no music, captions burned in, cut from
the launch film's clean composition or recorded on a real device with a real
library. Screenshots are real cards from a real library, with permission, at
2x, in light mode (the film's grade), with status bar cleaned.

**Link rule.** X suppresses the reach of posts that carry an outbound link. The
App Store link goes in the bio, in the pinned post, and in **the first comment**
under every post that ends with a call to action. Only three posts carry the
link in the body itself: the launch thread's last tweet, the thirty-day post,
and the close. Every link is a **campaign link** (§8) so App Store Connect can
attribute the install to the post that earned it.

throttles the reach of brand-new accounts, and an account with a finished
profile, a few weeks of age, and a real following graph gets seen. In those
three weeks: complete every profile field, follow 150 to 200 accounts in the
personal-knowledge, productivity, iOS-indie, and design space (the people whose
followers are Machina's first users), and reply thoughtfully to 3 to 5 posts a
day from those accounts. Replies only. Zero posts. The decision that the first
post is the launch post stands; the account arrives on Day 1 already looking
lived-in.

**Get X Premium for the account** before Day 1. Reply ranking, edit, and
analytics are each worth the fee on launch week alone. Premium also lifts the
character limit; the campaign ignores that and keeps every tweet under 280,
because a long post collapses behind "Show more" in the feed and the point of
every tweet here is that it is read whole.

## 3. When Day 1 is, and what to do if it moves

Day 1 is a **Tuesday**, and it is the day all six of these are true at once:

1. The App Store listing shows **Ready for Sale** and the link opens on a phone
   that is not signed into the developer account.
2. The Gemini spend cap is raised (SOURCE_OF_TRUTH §4 item 5b). At the current
   cap the app dies at roughly seventy active users, and a launch that goes
   well is exactly the day that happens.
3. The two Pro products are **Approved** in App Store Connect and a sandbox
   purchase, restore, and trial-to-free drop have each been run once on a
   fresh account (§7.1 mechanics). A Trust-week post about the price is a
   promise; the store must keep it.
4. The Product Hunt listing is scheduled for the same Tuesday, 12:01 AM Pacific.
5. The launch-week attachments (§6, A1 to A7) are rendered and in a folder.
6. The campaign links (§8) exist and each one opens the listing.

Tuesday because Product Hunt and X both peak Tuesday to Thursday, and because a
Tuesday launch puts the first Sunday synthesis post on Day 6, inside launch
week, where the ritual gets introduced while attention is highest. Avoid the
week of a US holiday and the week of an Apple event.

**Posting time: 9:00 AM Eastern** (16:00 Israel, 14:00 London, 06:00 Pacific)
on weekdays, **10:00 AM Eastern** on weekends. The audience for a productivity
app on X is heavily US, and 9:00 AM ET catches the East Coast at the desk and
the West Coast on the phone in bed, which is where a share-sheet app is
actually used. Schedule every post in advance; never post live from the phone
on launch day, when replies need all the attention.

**If App Review moves the date.** Nothing is posted until Ready for Sale is
true on a phone. The schedule is relative (Day 1, Day 2), so a slip moves the
whole campaign as a block: re-date every scheduled post to the new Tuesday,
move the Product Hunt slot, and keep replying from the account as before. Do
not post a "coming soon" to fill the gap; the first post is the launch post.
If the slip is more than two weeks, re-render A7 (the week-one grid) and
re-check §9 against whatever shipped meanwhile.

**Day 1 runbook (Eastern time).**

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 03:01 | Product Hunt listing is live; confirm the gallery and first comment rendered. |
| 06:00 | Confirm the App Store link opens on a non-developer phone. If not, stop; nothing posts today. |
| 08:55 | Switch the bio website to the `x-bio` campaign link. |
| 09:00 | T1 thread posts (scheduled). Pin T1.1 within a minute. Post the first comment under T1.5. |
| 09:05 | LinkedIn post (T1.1 text + A1). Tester email goes out. Press note goes out. |
| 09:00 to 18:00 | Reply to every reply within the hour. Screenshot over prose wherever one exists. |
| 21:00 | Note the day's numbers (impressions, profile visits, installs by campaign) in the log at the bottom of this file. |

## 4. The posts

Format: ID, day, weekday, then the post. IDs are internal (T1 to T31; the
launch thread is T1.1 to T1.5). Threads are shown one tweet per block, parts
separated by `~`.

Under each post, two lines:

- **Attach** is the one image or clip you attach to the tweet when you post
  it. Every tweet has exactly one; the numbered list of all of them is in §6.
- **First comment** is a short second post you write yourself, from the
  Machina account, as the first comment under your own tweet, right after it
  goes up. It is where the App Store link lives (X shows posts with an
  outbound link to fewer people, so the link goes one tap down) and where the
  obvious follow-up question gets answered before anyone asks it. "First
  comment: none" means post the tweet with its attachment and nothing else.

Character counts were checked by script (`verify.py` in this folder); every
tweet and every first comment is under 280.

## 5. The arcs, explained

Why arcs and singles and not a feature list:

- **Save from anywhere (week 2)** is the hero, so it gets the first arc and the
  only four-part one. The parts are the formats people actually save (article,
  video, screenshots) in rising order of surprise, and then the library they
  already own. Everyone expects an app to read an article. Fewer expect it to
  watch a video. Almost nobody expects five screenshots to become one read
  card, or ten years of bookmarks and a Pocket export to become a library that
  can be asked. The arc ends on the largest thing a follower has.
- **What a card knows (week 3)** answers the question week 2 raised: fine, it
  holds everything, but what does it do with it? Summary, filing, connections,
  in the order the product performs them, ending on the connection insight,
  which is the one thing a folder can never do.
- **Ask (week 4)** is deliberately fourth. Leading with Ask sells a chatbot,
  and every app has one. After three weeks of capture and understanding, Ask
  lands as the natural payoff, and the arc is built around trust rather than
  capability: only your library, a citation on every line, an honest no. Part
  3 is the one competitors cannot post.
- **Trust (week 5)** is where a curious follower becomes a user. Yours, free,
  everywhere: ownership, price, and reach, each one a reason not to hesitate.
  The price post is deliberately complete (the free numbers, the trial, the
  Pro price) because a price learned from a paywall feels like a trap and a
  price learned from a tweet feels like a fact.
- **The singles** carry the beats that need no chapter and keep the feed from
  reading as a manual: the problem (T2), the demo (T3), the anatomy (T4), the
  payoff (T6), week one (T7), search (T16), collections (T21), Hebrew (T26),
  sharing (T28), the belief (T29), the numbers (T30). Each is a complete
  thought and a complete screenshot.
- **The bigger idea (week 6)** turns outward. Sharing a card or a collection is
  the product's own growth loop; the belief post says the founder letter's
  line in the brand's voice; the numbers post is transparent by design; and
  the close repeats the launch promise, now earned.

The Sunday posts are the spine. They land on the day the product itself does
its weekly work, so the account's rhythm and the app's rhythm are the same
rhythm.

## 7. Engagement rules, the reply playbook, and the standing cadence

**Launch week.** Reply to every reply within the hour, for seven days. On X the
reply thread is the product demo for everyone who did not click. Answer
questions with a screenshot where one exists. Never argue. If someone compares
Machina to a competitor, say what Machina does, not what the other app fails
to do.

**Quote posts.** When a user posts a card, a synthesis, or an Ask answer, quote
it with one sentence and no link. User posts are the best content the account
will ever have, and quoting them trains people to post more.

**Never.** No engagement bait ("like if you agree"). No hashtags. No emoji. No
threads longer than five. No posting about competitors. No screenshots of
private libraries without written permission. No "AI-powered", "second brain",
"Machina AI"; "bookmarks" only as the name of a file you import. No em dashes,
including in replies. No promise of a feature that is not on the App Store
today.

**The reply playbook.** The twelve questions launch week will ask, with the
answer to give, each under 280 and each checked against the code on
2026-09-21. Paste, adjust the tone to the asker, never add a claim.
