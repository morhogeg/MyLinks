# Machina on X: the first 30 posts

> The launch campaign for the `@mymachina` account. Day 1 is App Store launch
> day. Six weeks, 30 posts, four feature series, one story. Brand voice, English
> only. Every product claim below was checked against the shipped code on
> 2026-09-02; the few that need a live screenshot or a real number are marked
> `[fill]` and must never be invented.
>
> House rules this document obeys, and every post must keep obeying: the name is
> `Machina` (never "Machina AI"); "second brain" and "AI-powered" appear nowhere;
> Machina is never called a bookmark manager; no em dashes anywhere, in posts or
> replies. The hero is **one place that holds everything you save** (BRANDING
> D-7). Ask is the payoff, not the lead. The tagline is
> **Everything you save, finally useful.** and it is used exactly three times
> in 30 posts, on purpose.

## 1. The strategy in one page

**What the campaign has to do.** Turn a launch into a habit of following. A
launch post gets one day of attention. A story gets six weeks of it. So the 30
posts are not 30 announcements. They are one argument, told in acts, with the
product demonstrating each step instead of describing it.

**The argument.**

1. You save things everywhere, and then you never see them again. (The problem
   is fragmentation, not clutter. This is the founder letter's story and the
   launch film's act one.)
2. Machina is one place for all of it, from every app, in one tap. (Capture.
   The hero.)
3. Every save is read, filed, and connected on its own. (Understanding. The
   thing no folder app does.)
4. So later you can just ask, and the answer is yours because the sources are.
   (Recall. The payoff.)
5. And you can trust it: your data, your library, free to start, honest when it
   doesn't know. (Trust. What turns a download into a habit.)

**The shape.** Six weeks. Week one is the launch: one post a day, each one
proving a different beat of the argument, so a new follower can read the
timeline top to bottom and understand the product. Weeks two to five each carry
a **three-day series** on one part of the product, numbered 1/3, 2/3, 3/3, so
the timeline reads like chapters and people come back for the next part.
Sundays belong to the **weekly synthesis**, the product's own Sunday ritual,
posted on the day it actually happens. Week six closes the loop: the growth
loop (shared cards), the belief, the numbers, the ask.

| Week | Arc | Days | Posts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Launch**: the whole argument, one beat a day | 1 to 8 | 1 to 7 |
| 2 | **Save from anywhere** (series: article, video, screenshots) | 9 to 13 | 8 to 11 |
| 3 | **What a card knows** (series: summary, filing, connections) | 15 to 20 | 12 to 16 |
| 4 | **Ask** (series: only your library, the citation, the honest no) | 22 to 27 | 17 to 21 |
| 5 | **Trust** (series: yours, free, everywhere) | 29 to 34 | 22 to 26 |
| 6 | **The bigger idea**: sharing, belief, the numbers, the close | 36 to 41 | 27 to 30 |

**Cadence.** Daily in launch week, then four posts a week (Tue, Wed, Thu series
days plus a weekend post), with Sundays reserved for synthesis-shaped posts.
That is dense enough to build a timeline and sparse enough that every post
carries a real screenshot or clip. Never post two feature posts on the same day.

**Voice.** Brand voice: plain, specific, confident, unhurried. Machina is the
subject of most sentences. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no hashtags, no
"we're thrilled". Short sentences. Concrete nouns (a sourdough schedule, a
40-minute talk) instead of categories ("content"). The product is never called
smart, magical, or powerful; the post shows what it did and lets the reader
draw the adjective. One `finally` per surface, so the tone stays wry and never
tips into scolding (BRANDING D-6).

**Media rule.** Every post ships with one image or one clip. A text-only post is
allowed exactly twice in 30 (the belief post and the community ask). Clips are
under 20 seconds, vertical or square, no music, captions burned in, cut from
the launch film's clean composition or recorded on a real device with a real
library. Screenshots are real cards from a real library, with permission, at
2x, in light mode (the film's grade), with status bar cleaned.

**Link rule.** X suppresses the reach of posts that carry an outbound link. The
App Store link goes in the bio, in the pinned post, and in **the first reply**
under every post that ends with a call to action. Only three posts carry the
link in the body itself: the launch post, the 30-day post, and the close.

## 3. When Day 1 is

Day 1 is a **Tuesday**, and it is the day all four of these are true at once:

1. The App Store listing shows **Ready for Sale** and the link opens on a phone
   that is not signed into the developer account.
2. The Gemini spend cap is raised (SOURCE_OF_TRUTH §4 item 5b). At the current
   cap the app dies at roughly seventy active users, and a launch that goes
   well is exactly the day that happens.
3. The Product Hunt listing is scheduled for the same Tuesday, 12:01 AM Pacific.
4. The launch-week assets (§6) are rendered and in a folder, all seven days.

Tuesday because Product Hunt and X both peak Tuesday to Thursday, and because a
Tuesday launch puts the first Sunday synthesis post on Day 6, inside launch
week, where the ritual gets introduced while attention is highest. Avoid the
week of a US holiday and the week of an Apple event.

**Posting time: 9:00 AM Eastern** (16:00 Israel, 14:00 London, 06:00 Pacific)
on weekdays, **10:00 AM Eastern** on weekends. The audience for a productivity
app on X is heavily US, and 9:00 AM ET catches the East Coast at the desk and
the West Coast on the phone in bed, which is where a share-sheet app is
actually used. Schedule every post in advance; never post live from the phone
on launch day, when replies need all the attention.

## 5. The arcs, explained

Why these series and not a feature list:

- **Save from anywhere (week 2)** is the hero, so it gets the first series. The
  three parts are the three formats people actually save (article, video,
  screenshots), in rising order of surprise. Everyone expects an app to read an
  article. Fewer expect it to watch a video. Almost nobody expects five
  screenshots to become one read card. The series ends on the strongest proof.
- **What a card knows (week 3)** answers the question week 2 raised: fine, it
  holds everything, but what does it do with it? Summary, filing, connections,
  in the order the product performs them, ending on the connection insight,
  which is the one thing a folder can never do and the App Store subtitle's
  original closing beat.
- **Ask (week 4)** is deliberately fourth. Leading with Ask sells a chatbot,
  and every app has one. After three weeks of capture and understanding, Ask
  lands as the natural payoff, and the series is built around trust rather
  than capability: only your library, a citation on every line, an honest no.
  Part 3 is the one competitors cannot post.
- **Trust (week 5)** is where a curious follower becomes a user. Yours, free,
  everywhere: ownership, price, and reach, each one a reason not to hesitate.
  The Hebrew post sits here because it is a trust signal too (the app was built
  for a real bilingual life, not a demo).
- **The bigger idea (week 6)** turns outward. Sharing a card is the product's
  own growth loop; the belief post says the founder letter's line in the brand's
  voice; the numbers post is transparent by design; and the close repeats the
  launch promise, now earned.

The Sunday posts are the spine. They land on the day the product itself does
its weekly work, so the account's rhythm and the app's rhythm are the same
rhythm. By week three a follower knows Sunday means synthesis.

## 7. Engagement rules and the standing cadence

**Launch week.** Reply to every reply within the hour, for seven days. On X the
reply thread is the product demo for everyone who did not click. Answer
questions with a screenshot where one exists. Never argue. If someone compares
Machina to a competitor, say what Machina does, not what the other app fails
to do.

**Quote posts.** When a user posts a card, a synthesis, or an Ask answer, quote
it with one sentence and no link. User posts are the best content the account
will ever have, and quoting them trains people to post more.

**Never.** No engagement bait ("like if you agree"). No hashtags. No emoji. No
threads longer than five. No posting about competitors. No screenshots of
private libraries without written permission. No "AI-powered", "second brain",
"bookmarks", "Machina AI". No em dashes, including in replies.

**After Post 30.** Three posts a week, indefinitely:

- **Tuesday:** one real card, one real save, one line of copy. The "look what
  it did with this" post.
- **Thursday:** one answer to a real question from the replies, or one small
  feature, shown in a clip.
- **Sunday:** synthesis-shaped. Once a month (first Sunday), the "What Machina
  learned this month" post from Post 26's format.

**Metrics that matter, checked weekly:** App Store installs attributed to X
(App Store Connect, source `x.com`), profile visits, and the reply count on
